Before you check

Assign your axes for each joint. For skew lines use the shortest common normal; for intersecting axes use the normal to their plane. Parallel or coincident axes allow the stated special choices.

Alternative consistent axis signs can be valid, but this worksheet grades the displayed instructor positive directions so the parameter table has one unique answer key. Reversing an axis requires recomputing the signed parameters. Use a consistent frame-sign convention throughout. After adding or removing any link or joint, recompute all frames and the parameter table.

Stage 2 · Complete the Modified D–H table

Use the joint geometry and the frames you assigned. Enter aᵢ₋₁, αᵢ₋₁, dᵢ, θᵢ for every joint. Write θᵢ for revolute variables and dᵢ for prismatic variables—do not expand them as q offsets.

Units: lengths in metres, angles in degrees. You may use numeric constants (for example 1.75, −90) and symbols (θ₂, d₃). The teacher solution stays hidden until you check. A prismatic joint can have a nonzero constant θᵢ even though its variable is dᵢ. In this reference configuration, θ₃ = −90°.

Frame-assignment and simplification checklist

  1. Choose zi from joint motion: zi must lie along the actual rotation or translation axis of joint i.
  2. Choose xi from the next joint: it is perpendicular to both zi and zi+1, not chosen from the previous joint.
  3. Skew axes: use the shortest common perpendicular between the two axis lines.
  4. Intersecting axes: choose xi normal to the plane spanned by the axes; apply the right-hand sign convention.
  5. Parallel but distinct axes: point xi along a perpendicular connector between them.
  6. Coincident axes: choose a convenient common perpendicular and preserve a consistent sign.
  7. Place origins on z: Oi must lie on its own zi axis. Slide origins along that axis only when the complete D–H geometry remains consistent.
  8. Reduce unnecessary constants: use a shared origin where multiple joint-axis lines truly intersect. Never introduce an invisible graphical mounting offset.
  9. Maintain right-handed frames: yi is implicitly zi × xi; only red z and green x are drawn.
  10. Ground and gripper: fix O₀, x₀, z₀; the end-effector keeps the orientation of the last joint frame, though its tool origin may be offset.
  11. Physical L is not automatically D–H a or d: determine a, α, d, θ using the assigned frames, including their signs and actual origins.
  12. Write joint variables symbolically: θi for R and di for P. Define zero offsets separately; do not substitute expanded q expressions.
  13. Recompute after every structural change: adding/deleting/reorienting a joint or link must refresh frames, origin choices, table rows and student grading.
  14. Check geometric consistency: if chosen origins cause a nonzero MDH translation residual, the resulting parameter row is provisional and must be reviewed.
Suggested student sequence: identify joint axes → assign x/z → simplify origins → calculate a, α, d, θ → check and review feedback.
